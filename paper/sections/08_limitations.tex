\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
Our certified models use Boolean tasks rather than naturally occurring language-model
computations. Their depth certificates depend on the specified interventions,
strictness constraints and bypass tests; backup computation outside the designated
circuit remains a threat to interpretation. The reduced one-day experimental grid
uses $L=D$, $k\in\{4,8,16\}$ and 100 graph examples, limiting the resolution of
hyperparameter and seed comparisons. Any claims about the depth trend must be
interpreted against the completed, merged Boolean results rather than the pilot
numbers alone.

Our GPT-2 validation is small: 10 retained two-hop prompts and 12 one-hop
controls, selected using an answer-rank threshold of 50. State-paired depth
contrasts and causal-specificity tests have only six and seven usable observations,
respectively. Depth sensitivity is supported for PLT but not CLT, whereas causal
specificity is supported for CLT but not PLT. Graph--intervention correlations
are positive but inconclusive. The absolute-normalised depth measure also
changes the interpretation of V1. These outcomes do not establish a universal
depth-collapse effect in pretrained language models.

The GPT-2 evaluation uses previously trained transcoders with the corrected
validation code. Although the Hugging Face and generic-engine MLP-input
consistency check passes, the original and corrected training implementations
are not identical. The PLT has 37.8 million parameters and the CLT has
141.6 million; their reconstruction errors therefore do not constitute a
parameter-matched architectural comparison. The transcoders are small relative
to production systems, and attribution graphs retain substantial skip and
error-node influence. InterpBench was not run.

Taken together, the study distinguishes reconstructing a computation from
faithfully representing its causal structure. Certified-depth experiments
provide controlled tests of graph fidelity, while the mixed GPT-2 results
highlight the need for larger, parameter-controlled real-model evaluations.
